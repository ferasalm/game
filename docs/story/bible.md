## Story promise

In a cyberpunk Old Sana’a where clean water is rationed and acidic rain makes the streets dangerous, a disgraced covert operative escapes prison with help from a masked cyborg dancer. He wants revenge on the security chief who secretly ordered his parents killed. The escape draws him into a city already breaking under corruption, rebellion, and a dangerous technology that can disrupt bodies and machines.

The emotional engine is mistaken identity and corrupted trust. Rydan looks like a ruthless villain and treats people as tools; Bilal believes Rydan murdered him; Zayd is an honest rookie caught inside a corrupt police case; Roya is a survivor whose losses become political resolve; Bilqis seems to possess impossible powers but uses technology, training, and a dangerous system whose full history remains mysterious.

Central question: when institutions control water, information, bodies, and truth, can people fight back without becoming another instrument of control?

Ordinary prosthetic limbs do not remove thirst, hunger, or the body's water needs. Extensive closed-loop life-support systems might reduce some needs, but still rely on supplies, maintenance, and heat management. Scarcity must remain meaningful.

## World and visual language

Old Sana’a remains recognizably itself rather than becoming a generic neon city. Historic tower houses, carved façades, narrow lanes, roof bridges, markets, and qamariya windows have been repaired or crowded with cables, condensers, sensors, and signs. Colored light spills across wet stone and metal when power or rain makes it glow.

Water is infrastructure and leverage. A household may be charged separately for water, power, medical upkeep, and shelter. Control of a cistern, purification unit, delivery corridor, or ration ledger can matter more than a weapon. Energy is easier to obtain than water, but devices still need cells, parts, cooling, and authorized access.

Acid rain shapes ordinary behavior. People carry protective umbrellas, wear sealed layers, inspect exposed joints, and shelter in tayramana rooms. A tayramana is a refuge with colored qamariya light, herbs, and qishr: a warm domestic space that contrasts with the hostile street. Rainwater is not automatically safe to drink; it must be collected and treated.

Cyborg conversion is common and often chosen for labor, protection, medical need, or status. It does not make people invulnerable or self-sufficient. A damaged prosthesis can mean lost work; a locked implant can become a leash; a black-market repair can be lifesaving and risky.

Suggested time-period language: 'late century' or 'the ration era' until the technology, climate, and political history justify a specific year. The city can feel future-facing without a date label.

## Making apparent magic legible as science fiction

| Effect people call magic | Grounded story explanation | Limits that preserve tension |
|---|---|---|
| Bilqis seems to read a crowd | She combines observation, public records, wearable telemetry, and probabilistic signal analysis. She infers stress, affiliation, and likely behavior; she does not read minds. | The inference can be wrong, especially with offline, masked, or emotionally controlled people. Show uncertainty. |
| A dancer controls or hypnotizes a room | Choreography, lighting, sound, social cues, and timed exploits of compatible implants make a crowd appear synchronized. Some follow knowingly; others are distracted or have vulnerable hardware. | No universal remote control of unmodified minds. Distance, noise, incompatible devices, and informed resistance reduce the effect. |
| People move like zombies | A crisis can combine impaired judgment or motor coordination in living people with compromised prosthetics and implants. Witnesses call them dead or possessed in panic. | No true resurrection. A clinically dead body cannot return to coordinated life. Movement after brain death must come visibly from powered prosthetics or an external rig. |
| A catastrophic virus affects biology and cybernetics | Treat it as a paired bio-cyber threat: a biological agent affects living tissue, while a digital payload compromises compatible implants and connected infrastructure. Cyborgs can be vulnerable to both; unaugmented people only to the biological hazard; isolated machines only to the digital one. | It is not one germ infecting silicon. It needs a delivery system, compatible targets, and vulnerable systems. Exact origin, scope, and countermeasure are OPEN. Keep it fictional and non-operational. |
| Bilqis appears to unleash the city | Her capability may be access to a legacy emergency network or prepared release system, not a spell she conjures. Rydan might provide access, protect her from security, or supply a physical key. | She cannot trigger it anywhere at no cost. Define the site, prerequisites, risk to allies, and a way to stop or limit it before the reveal. |
| Jambiya slots give special abilities | Slots hold engineered modules: compact capacitors, sensor packages, edge treatments, or haptic/aim-assist components. The hilt routes power and feedback; the blade remains a physical tool. | Slots do not create energy. Modules draw charge, heat up, wear down, and need maintenance. The weapon remains a jambiya in silhouette and handling. |

The hybrid-virus idea is a strong catastrophic threat but is not yet a confirmed plot mechanism. A useful ethical conflict is that Bilqis may be able to stop a system-wide attack only by activating a system whose effects she cannot predict. Rydan's role could be to contain, redirect, or shut it down rather than simply authorize mass harm.

The references at the end support only narrow real-world analogies—water recovery, medical-device cybersecurity, human water balance, and prosthetic sensory feedback. They do not validate crowd-scale mind control or a city-wide paired bio-cyber agent.

## Prototype and demo structure

| Order | Sequence | Purpose and playable focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Roya cold-open fragment (non-playable) | A vivid glimpse of Roya acting against surveillance or ration control during a crisis. Show her as capable and guarded. End on a threat or interruption; withhold her history. Revisit this flash-forward later. |
| 2 | Rydan escapes prison (playable tutorial) | Bilqis assists from outside through a compatible system or diversion. Rydan begins with the one-slot prison jambiya. Teach movement, combat, cover, and a simple tech interaction. Zayd and several inmates get caught up in the escape; Rydan lets Zayd think he is leading while pursuing his own goal. |
| 3 | TITLE: Earlier — Zayd's first shift | Rewind clearly. Follow rookie officer Zayd on his first Old Sana’a shift. A masked gang causes a disturbance. Corrupt officers disagree over whether to let them escape; Zayd intervenes and helps keep them from walking free. |
| 4 | Zayd's arrest and prison arrival | The gang case becomes a separate police-corruption case. Evidence, witnesses, or chain of custody is manipulated. Zayd is imprisoned; his story explains how he reached the prison where Rydan is held. |
| 5 | Return to the escape / catch-up | Return to the tutorial's escape at a clear visual beat, without replaying it. Continue forward with Zayd and Rydan's uneasy partnership. |
| 6 | Demo endpoint | End on a decisive escape and a new objective: reach a contact, expose a security lie, or find a route toward Numan. Tease Bilqis's larger capability without explaining it. Save Roya's full origin and Numan's death for later. |

Dramatic framing: the cold open raises a question; the tutorial gives immediate agency; Zayd's rewind changes the meaning of the prison cast. An on-screen 'Earlier' card and repeated detail—such as the same alarm light or Qamariya reflection—make the chronology legible.

For this demo, keep the playable viewpoint on Rydan. Bilqis can assist remotely or appear in a brief cutaway. Zayd is an ally and narrative lens, not a second protagonist who displaces the combat tutorial.

## Editable prose treatment

### Opening Fragment Roya

Acid rain drums on a roof above Old Sana’a. Through a band of colored qamariya glass, Roya watches a ration checkpoint below: guards, a queue, and a delivery cart that should have gone to a clinic. Her face is partly hidden; the audience catches a glimpse of a prosthetic hand or rebel signal, but not enough to explain who she is. A system alert begins to identify her. She makes a choice that protects someone in the queue and exposes her position. Cut to black before the consequence is clear.

This is a teaser, not her full origin. Later, when the story reaches the same rebel operation, the player sees why Roya is there and what she has lost. Her birth status and the sheikh's adoption are reserved for that later account.

### Rydan Prison Escape

Rydan is held in a prison that has become part of the city's security machine. He is accused of killing his covert-operations partner Bilal and helping a foreign woman enter the country. The accusation is politically useful and emotionally credible: Bilal escaped alive, but authorities and Bilal have been led to believe Rydan betrayed him. Rydan knows the official story is false, but does not yet know the full role his brother Kanan played.

An interruption hits the prison: power fluctuations, alarms, and confusion that appear coordinated. Bilqis, hidden behind a dancer's public identity, is helping from outside. She does not simply turn every guard into a puppet. She creates openings by interfering with compatible systems, timing diversions, and exploiting what the prison already controls. Rydan fights through the escape using a one-slot jambiya, choosing when to use force and when to use the environment.

A rookie prisoner, Zayd, and several inmates get swept into the escape. Rydan understands the layout and quietly directs events, while allowing Zayd to think he is taking charge. Rydan's apparent selflessness is ambiguous: he may be protecting people, using them as cover, or both. Bilqis's help gets them out, but leaves a trace security can follow.

### Earlier Zayd First Day

The story rewinds to Zayd's first shift as a new officer in Old Sana’a. A masked gang triggers a public disturbance around a contested street or delivery route. The gang's motive is open; a strong option is extorting control over a water corridor. Zayd arrives expecting ordinary procedure and discovers officers willing to let the gang go, erase evidence, or protect a patron.

Zayd tries to do the right thing and influences a few officers to keep the gang from escaping. His intervention makes enemies. The ensuing case is a separate instance of police corruption: records are altered, a witness is pressured, or evidence is planted to make Zayd look culpable. He is arrested and sent to the prison where Rydan is already held. His chronology catches up to the tutorial.

### Roya Full History for Later Reveal

Roya is born into the Mzayyin/Mzayyinah status described by the user: a hereditary social category associated with beautifying and service trades, lack of tribal lineage, and stigma that can persist across generations. A young man from a tribal family loves her, but his relatives threaten the couple because marriage across the status boundary is taboo. They run toward another village and are stopped at a checkpoint.

The local sheikh hears their account and adopts Roya. In the working version, the adoption gives her a lasting recognized change of status, allowing the marriage to proceed. This is not a claim that the sheikh dies because of adopting her. On the wedding day, a gang attacks. The sheikh is killed; Roya's husband is abducted and badly beaten. The sheikh's nephew and villagers fight the gang off and help the injured groom. The couple marry, then leave for Old Sana’a because the village is no longer safe and the injured husband has no one able to protect them.

In the city, their savings disappear into rent, water, electricity, and medical treatment. A landlord or broker charges them for essential services. The husband's injuries cause delayed complications; he eventually dies. Roya is left poor, grieving, and a witness to corruption in the village, street, and hospital systems. She is not a villain. Her anger becomes political, and she joins or aids a rebel movement. Her methods may become ruthless, putting her in uneasy alignment with Bilqis.

A two-stage political connection is the current proposal: the gang attacks for its own reason, perhaps to dominate a water route; later Numan's government uses disorder and public fear to justify taking direct control of that route. The government did not order the original attack unless that is chosen later.

### Rydan Numan and the Frame Up

As a child, Rydan loses his parents in an operation secretly ordered by Numan, then head of a covert security unit. Rydan does not know Numan caused their deaths when he later joins the unit. His family may have been connected to an outlawed technical tradition that outsiders call witchcraft; whether the family had a rare skill, hidden archive, or only a reputation remains open. Rydan himself is not supernatural.

Rydan grows into a precise, agile fighter who relies on observation, timing, and a jambiya rather than size. He is recruited into covert operations and works with Bilal. His brother Kanan manipulates secret orders and evidence. Kanan makes Bilal believe Rydan is dangerous and makes Rydan believe Bilal is dead or has betrayed him. Rydan is framed for Bilal's supposed murder and for assisting a foreign woman—Bilqis—into the country. Bilal escaped alive; Rydan did not kill him.

Rydan's parents' murder and the Bilal frame-up can be connected through Numan's unit, but the exact chain of command is open. The useful reveal is that Rydan served for years without knowing that his institution was responsible for his family's destruction.

### Numan Fall Bilal Return and Kanan Exposure

Rydan's pursuit brings him into conflict with Numan. The climax is a boss encounter and moral choice, not only a revenge cutscene. Rydan kills Numan, as currently intended. The government declares emergency rule; surveillance tightens and street fighting spreads through the Qamariya-lit city. Rydan and Bilqis become two of the most wanted people, with dead-only bounties.

During the bounty hunt, Bilal returns as a boss opponent. He believes Rydan is the traitor who tried to kill him. Their fight comes from Kanan's lie, not a simple villain reveal. A three-way encounter among Rydan, Bilal, and Kanan exposes the manipulation. Kanan is accidentally provoked; his pride makes him correct or reveal a detail about a forged order that only the person who altered it could know. Rydan and Bilal recognize the misunderstanding and turn against Kanan's scheme.

Kanan's fate remains open. Options include Rydan killing him, Bilal killing him, or Kanan escaping after exposure. The misunderstanding can be resolved before this choice, so reconciliation does not depend on Kanan's death.

## Rydan Three Jambiya Progression

This equipment structure is confirmed. Rydan owns three complete jambiyas, each made of a hilt and a blade. There are exactly three hilts in the game. Different blades can provide variety while remaining compatible with those hilts. The hilt determines module capacity; a slot holds a technology module, not a magical gemstone.

| Acquisition | Hilt capacity | Story meaning / gameplay |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Prison escape | One slot | Rydan begins the playable game with this hilt. It is a practical, limited weapon acquired in or through the prison. It teaches the basic module system. |
| 2. After defeating Numan | Two slots | A major upgrade after the covert chief's defeat. Origin—captured weapon, recovered cache, or an item from Numan's armory—is open. |
| 3. Grandfather's grave and restored family house/heritage | Three slots | The family heirloom associated with Rydan's grandfather. Finding the grave and restoring the house makes inheritance a story achievement, not just loot. |
| Much later: chain-linked form | All three hilts linked | Rydan attaches the three complete jambiyas with a chain to form a new combined weapon. This is well after the demo and should feel earned, powerful, and harder to control. |

Slots could accept engineered modules that alter edge vibration, power delivery, haptic feedback, sensors, or another legible function. Blade choice and hilt capacity provide separate variety. The chain weapon should trade reach and multi-angle attacks for greater weight, entanglement risk, slower recovery, or high energy demand. Module names, effects, and balance remain open.
